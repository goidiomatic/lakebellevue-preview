What brings people here

Sometimes life asks more of us than we can sort through alone.

People come to counseling for many reasons. Some are carrying worry or pressure that won’t settle. Some feel flat, discouraged, or disconnected from the people and things that used to matter. Others are in the middle of a change, a relationship that has become difficult, or a quiet sense that life could feel more like their own.

You don’t need to have the words for it yet, or be in crisis, to begin. Counseling offers a steady, confidential space to slow down, understand what is happening, and find practical next steps that fit your life.

Approach

A more thoughtful way forward

Counseling here is collaborative, grounded, and focused on what will actually help in your day-to-day life.

  1. Understanding first

    We take time to understand your experience in context: what you’re feeling, what has shaped it, and what matters to you. Clarity often begins with being genuinely heard.

  2. Practical and real-life

    Insight is most useful when it changes how a Tuesday feels. Sessions balance reflection with concrete tools and next steps you can bring into work, relationships, and home.

  3. Collaborative and respectful

    You are the expert on your own life. Goals are set together, the pace is yours, and the work adapts as you do.

  4. Toward meaning, not only relief

    Feeling better matters. So does living in a way that reflects your values. Counseling can help with both.

Good Faith Estimate

You have the right to receive a “Good Faith Estimate” explaining how much your medical care will cost.

Under the law, health care providers need to give patients who don’t have insurance or who are not using insurance an estimate of the bill for medical items and services.

  • You have the right to receive a Good Faith Estimate for the total expected cost of any non-emergency items or services. This includes related costs like medical tests, prescription drugs, equipment, and hospital fees.
  • Make sure your health care provider gives you a Good Faith Estimate in writing at least 1 business day before your medical service or item. You can also ask your health care provider, and any other provider you choose, for a Good Faith Estimate before you schedule an item or service.
  • If you receive a bill that is at least $400 more than your Good Faith Estimate, you can dispute the bill.
  • Make sure to save a copy or picture of your Good Faith Estimate.

For questions or more information about your right to a Good Faith Estimate, visit www.cms.gov/nosurprises or call 1-800-985-3059.
